# P29 — Protection against exposure, cloning and reverse engineering

## Purpose

Audit and reduce exposure of code, commercial rules, secrets, data models, prompts, integrations and released artifacts. The goal is to raise the cost of cloning and reduce the impact of a copy without promising invulnerability.

## Core rule

Anything delivered to a browser, app or client device must be treated as inspectable. Secrets, authorization, critical calculations and strategic rules stay on the server.

## Required flow

`INVENTORY → AUDIT → CORRECT → HARDEN → VERIFY → TEST → RELEASE`

## Checks

- secrets, tokens, keys and public variables;
- source maps, debug files and internal messages;
- critical logic executed only on the client;
- authentication, authorization and rate limiting;
- dependencies, scripts and global changes;
- build hashes, signing and traceability;
- compatibility and impact of hardening tools;
- rollback and recovery of the original artifact.

## BLA — controlled use

Blind LLM Agents (BLA) may be evaluated as an optional layer for first-party artifacts, especially compiled JavaScript, Electron, WASM, binaries and models. Separate empirical validation against REA from internal estimates. Do not install globally, protect production directly, or treat BLA as a replacement for server-side architecture.

## Acceptance criteria

- no secret in the client;
- source maps private or removed from release;
- critical rules enforced on the server;
- functional and security tests pass;
- original artifact preserved;
- hash and version recorded;
- rollback proven;
- evidence classified as PASS, FAIL, NOT_RUN or INCONCLUSIVE.

## Limit

No client-side protection completely prevents human analysis or copying of a public application. The release decision must record residual risks.
